## Angle
The country's name looks timeless, but its official adoption has a documented date and a negotiation behind it:
a new dynasty (Nguyễn, 1802) asked the Qing for recognition under one name, the Qing objected, and a reordered name came back.
Twist for the objective tone: the words "Việt" and "Nam" were each old, and (research) "Việt Nam" may appear in Vietnamese writings before 1804;
the name was also replaced later (Đại Nam, Annam under France) and only became the everyday state name in the 20th century.
The video corrects two simple stories at once: "China named Vietnam" and "the name is thousands of years old".

## Perspectives (≥ 2)
- **Qing side**: Qing veritable records (清實錄, Jiaqing reign) / Qing edicts on the investiture — why "Nam Việt" was refused (research the stated reason).
- **Nguyễn side**: *Đại Nam thực lục* (Nguyễn court chronicle) — how Gia Long's court presented the request and the result.
- **Modern historians**: A. Woodside, *Vietnam and the Chinese Model* (1971); K. W. Taylor, *A History of the Vietnamese* (2013); research also
  L. Kelley, *Beyond the Bronze Pillars* (2005) on envoy practice; Britannica "Vietnam: history". Chinese- or Vietnamese-language scholarship as pointer only.

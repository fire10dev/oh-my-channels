## Angle
"What the sources actually say" (the L003 format, our best long: 72 views, CTR 1.52%) + a detective cold open.
The question the video keeps open: **was Nanyue a Chinese kingdom, a Vietnamese kingdom, or neither?** The answer we land on is the scholarly one:
the labels "China" and "Vietnam" did not exist yet; the Yue people and a Qin general made something that both later nations read back into their own story.

## Tone rules
- Mystery first: a tomb, a gold seal, a title that should not exist. No battle-led opening (L001 kept 5%: viewers left at once).
- **Say "Nanyue" and "Vietnam" in the first 10 s.** Hook rule from S013: the strange object is only a clue; the kingdom is the subject.
- Objective: give the Vietnamese view, the Chinese view and the modern Guangdong/Cantonese view their own words, each attributed. We never pick a winner.
- Legend is labelled as legend on screen (`LEGEND` tag): the magic crossbow, Mỵ Châu and Trọng Thủy, Zhao Tuo's lifespan.
- No modern politics: no South China Sea, no 1979, no border disputes, no "who owns Guangdong today".
- No victory framing. The Han conquest of 111 BC is told as an ending, not as a triumph or a tragedy for "Vietnam".

## Research targets (verify each; cut anything weak)
Primary sources (in translation): *Shiji* 113 (Nanyue liezhuan) and 97 (Lu Jia); *Hanshu* 95; *Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư* (Ngoại kỷ, Triệu kỷ); *Lĩnh Nam chích quái* (crossbow legend).
Secondary: Taylor, *The Birth of Vietnam* (1983) · Brindley, *Ancient China and the Yue* (2015) · Kiernan, *Việt Nam: A History* (2017) ·
Kelley (Lê Minh Khải blog/articles on Zhao Tuo historiography) · Nanyue King Mausoleum museum + Nanyue palace site (Guangzhou) · Cổ Loa excavation reports.
- T1 Tomb of Zhao Mo: found 1983, Xianggang hill, Guangzhou; jade suit; gold seal 文帝行璽 "Emperor Wen's seal" (check reading, weight, which king).
- T2 Qin southern campaign year (214 BC?) and the three commanderies.
- T3 Ren Xiao → Zhao Tuo handover; Nanyue founding year (204/203 BC — "around" unless sources agree).
- T4 Lu Jia's 196 BC and 179 BC missions; exact wording of Zhao Tuo's letter (who translates it, how).
- T5 Empress Lü's trade ban: goods named, year; Zhao Tuo's emperor title; Changsha raid.
- T6 Âu Lạc: what Shiji/Hanshu actually say (bribes/"役屬"?); legend sources and their dates; Cổ Loa arrowhead finds (year, count).
- T7 Zhao Tuo's death 137 BC and claimed age (tradition only).
- T8 Queen Jiu, Anguo Shaoji, Lü Jia, 112 BC killings, 111 BC fall, nine commanderies (names + which lie in Vietnam).
- T9 Historiography: Lê Văn Hưu 1272, Ngô Thì Sĩ, modern Vietnamese view (which historians, when); Chinese view; Guangdong heritage use. Quote scholars, not forums.
- T10 Cantonese/Yue name link (粵/越) — only if a scholarly source supports it; otherwise cut.

## Sensitive points
- Never say "Zhao Tuo was Vietnamese" or "Nanyue was Chinese" as narrator fact. Always "X wrote / Y argues".
- The crossbow story is legend; the archaeology of Cổ Loa is separate evidence and must not be presented as proof of the legend.
- Lifespans, army sizes, dates before 179 BC: give ranges or "traditionally".
- Do not show modern flags or modern borders on maps. Maps use "Han commanderies", "Nanyue", "Âu Lạc (Red River)".

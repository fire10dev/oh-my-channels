## Framing decision
- Question kept open to the end: **was Nanyue Chinese, Vietnamese, or neither?** Landing = the scholars' answer (C31): the labels did not exist yet; both later traditions read their story into it. We never pick a winner.
- Spine (chosen after brainstorm, table below): **"two names"** — King Wen in the books / Emperor Wen on the seal (C3–C4); king to Han / emperor at home (C16);
  Zhao Tuo / Triệu Đà; record vs legend (ch. 4); founder vs invader (ch. 6); Nam Việt → Việt Nam (ch. 7).
- Perspectives, each attributed: Han histories (Sima Qian, Ban Gu), Vietnamese chronicle + legend (Toàn thư, Lĩnh Nam chích quái), Lê Văn Hưu vs Ngô Thì Sĩ vs 20th-c. Vietnamese historians,
  Guangzhou museum (local Lingnan heritage), modern scholars (Taylor, Brindley, Kim).
- Legend (C18) is tagged on screen with `SRC:LEGEND …`. Cổ Loa archaeology (C20) is said **not** to prove the legend.
- No modern politics (no borders, no sea, no wars after 1804, no "who owns Guangdong"). 111 BC told as an ending: "the kingdom was gone".
- Two men called "Jia": the envoy **Lu Jia** (陸賈) and the minister **Lü Jia** (呂嘉). Narration always says "the envoy Lu Jia" / "the minister Lü Jia".

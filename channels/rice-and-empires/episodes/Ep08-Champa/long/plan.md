## Owner direction (2026-11-11 16:53 +07)
- Tell it **grand and regretful**: a great sea kingdom of more than a thousand years, then gone as a state. The fact: Đại Việt ended Champa.
- **Sad ending**, with the line "history has no *if*". No "what if Champa had survived" speculation.
- **The last ~45 s turn bright**: the empire ended, but the people and their culture did not. Cham culture and Cham food are part of Vietnam today.

## Tone rules
- Grand: chapters 0–3 use wide shots, temples, sea and trade, with music that builds.
- Regret: chapters 5–6 use slow pacing, empty towers, the sea at dusk and quiet music. Regret comes from images and pacing, **not from blaming anyone**.
- Objective:
  - Show Champa also attacking Đại Việt (Chế Bồng Nga 1371) and fighting the Khmer, so the video is not a victim film.
  - Use no "genocide/massacre" wording unless a scholarly source uses it, and then attribute it.
- Bright ending: **both things are true**. Cham communities keep their own identity (Bani/Balamon, the Cham script, Katê), and Cham culture has also entered wider Vietnamese culture.
  Never say "they assimilated and disappeared".

## Research targets for chapter 7 (verify each; cut anything weak)
- Bàu Trúc Cham pottery on UNESCO's Urgent Safeguarding List (check the year, believed to be 2022).
- Katê festival: national intangible heritage listing (year?).
- Po Nagar / Thiên Y A Na: Vietnamese worship at a Cham temple (scholarly source: e.g. Taylor, or Nguyễn Thế Anh).
- Cham influence on central Vietnamese cuisine, music and vocabulary. Only items backed by ≥ 2 good sources. **No folk "everyone knows" claims** (e.g. "fish sauce came from Champa" is unsourced → cut unless sourced).
- Cham dishes today (e.g. in Ninh Thuận / An Giang). Show them as living Cham food, not only as "absorbed into Vietnamese food".

## Sensitive points
- 1471 numbers: sources differ hugely (C8). Use ranges and attribution only.
- 1832 annexation and Minh Mạng's policies: describe them historically, with the source named.
- No modern politics (no FULRO, no land disputes, no current policy).
- The video says Champa "ended as a state". It never says "the Cham disappeared".

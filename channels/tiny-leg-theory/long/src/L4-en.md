# L4 How to Study So It Actually Sticks

### s26 [buff] Rereading Isn't Learning — fluency illusion
- You reread your notes three times. Easy reps.
- They look familiar, so it feels like learning.
- Familiar isn't remembering.
- Close the book. Say it from memory.
- Then check what you missed.
- Pause this. Say today's lesson out loud.

### s27 [pen] The Study Trick That Feels Harder — retrieval practice
- Testing yourself feels worse than rereading.
- You struggle. You get things wrong.
- But pulling ideas out of memory helps them stick.
- Flashcards, practice questions, explain it out loud.
- Hard now, easier on the test.
- What will you quiz yourself on today?

### s73 [pen] Why You Judge a Day by Its Ending — Peak-end rule
- A genuinely good day. Then one awkward text at 11 p.m.
- And now the whole day feels ruined.
- Memory doesn't replay every hour. It keeps a few snapshots.
- Mostly the strongest moment, and how things ended.
- It's often called the peak-end rule.
- Before bed, write down three moments that went fine.
- And let the last few minutes be something small and kind.
- One bad text was one minute. You lived all the others too.

### s74 [buff] Don't Confuse Effort With Progress — Effort heuristic
- Four highlighter colors. Every page glowing. No idea what's on it.
- It felt hard, so it must be working. Sure, buddy.
- We tend to judge value by sweat, not by results.
- Before the set, name the result. After it, check you got it.
- Tired isn't done. Done is done.

### s75 [pen] Why You Feel Like a Beginner Again — Skill acquisition stages
- New software at work. Everyone clicks fast. You hunt for buttons.
- It's easy to decide this just isn't your thing.
- Early on, every single step needs your full attention.
- With practice, steps tend to chain together and get quieter.
- So skip judging the whole skill. Pick one small piece.
- Which piece would make tomorrow a little less clumsy?

### s76 [buff] Use a Checkpoint, Not a Pep Talk — Feedback control
- Forty slides done. Grading sheet never opened.
- That's lifting with no mirror. Form goes sideways.
- A pep talk makes you push harder. Not in the right direction.
- Feedback works by comparing where you are to the target.
- Halfway through, open the criteria. Tick each one.
- Missing one? Fix it now, not the night before.
- Check the mirror mid-set, or redo the whole set. Pick.

### s77 [pen] Why You Can't Explain What You Know — Illusion of explanatory depth
- You know how a zipper works. Until someone asks you to explain it.
- Then the words run out after one sentence.
- We often feel we understand things in more detail than we do.
- Try writing the steps one by one, with nothing to look up.
- The gaps you find aren't failures. They're the map.

### s78 [buff] Mix the Problem Types — Interleaving effect
- Twenty problems in a row, same type. You crushed them.
- Then the exam mixes them up.
- Same-type sets hand you the method before you start.
- The real skill is spotting which method this one needs.
- So shuffle the set. Mix related types together.
- It feels slower and messier. It tends to stick better.
- Before solving, say the method out loud. Then go.
- Same machine every rep is comfy. Mixed sets win exams.

### s79 [pen] Why You Forget What You Just Read — Levels of processing
- You finished the chapter. You can't recall a single idea.
- Your eyes moved down every line. Your mind stayed on the surface.
- Memory tends to hold what we process for meaning.
- Meaning grows when a new idea hooks onto one you already know.
- After each section, link it to one example from your own life.
- So what does this idea remind you of?

### s80 [buff] Teach It in Plain Words — Self-explanation effect
- Copied the solution. Every step. No idea why step three works.
- Copying is watching someone else lift.
- Explaining why each step works makes you do the rep.
- It tends to expose gaps and tie each step to its rule.
- Cover the textbook. Say each step in plain words.
- Stuck on one? That's your weak spot. Hit it.
- Explain it, you own it. Copy it, you rent it.

### s81 [pen] Why a Blank Page Helps You Remember — Generation effect
- Try to recall before you peek.
- Words you produce yourself tend to stick better than words you only read.
- Reading hands you the answer. Producing it makes your mind build the path.
- So close the notebook and write what you remember on a blank page.
- Then open it, check, and fix the gaps in a different color.
- The gaps are your next lesson. What will you write down first?

### s82 [buff] Don't Memorize the Worked Example — Transfer appropriate processing
- You memorized the worked example. Every step. Nice.
- Then the exam asks it a different way. Blank.
- You trained one move. The test wants a different lift.
- Recall works best when practice looks like the real task.
- So train with fresh questions, not the same example again.
- Switch the numbers. Switch the wording. Solve it cold.
- One new problem tonight. That's your set.

### s83 [pen] Why You Remember the Weird Example — Distinctiveness effect
- You forgot the definition, but you still remember the teacher's weird example.
- Ordinary examples tend to blur into each other.
- Something odd stands out, so it is easier to find again later.
- That's the distinctiveness effect.
- So when an idea feels abstract, give it a strange, specific example.
- Opportunity cost? The concert you skipped so you could pay rent.
- Odd is fine. It still has to be accurate.
- The idea might fade. The strange picture tends to bring it back.

### s84 [buff] Stop Studying Only in One Format — Encoding specificity
- You know the word on the flashcard. Someone says it out loud. Nothing.
- Memory gets tied to the cues you learned it with.
- One format, one set of cues. Change the cue, and it may not show up.
- So mix the reps. Say it, write it, use it in a sentence.
- Five words, three formats, tonight. Flipping cards is not a full set.

### s85 [pen] Why Cramming Fades So Fast — Spacing effect
- You crammed all night, passed the quiz, and lost it by next week.
- Cramming can carry you to tomorrow. Then it tends to fade fast.
- The same hours spread across days usually hold much longer.
- That's the spacing effect. A small gap makes each review count more.
- Split one long session into short ones on different days.
- Today, then in a couple of days, then again next week.
- It feels slower going in. It also tends to stay.

### s86 [buff] Make the Answer Before You See It — Pretesting effect
- Guess first. The answer will land harder.
- Reading the whole chapter first feels safe. Sure, buddy.
- Even a wrong guess primes you to spot the answer when it shows up.
- That's the pretesting effect. A warm-up before the real set.
- Hit the end-of-chapter questions before you read. Then read.
- Next chapter: three questions cold. Guess loud, then read.

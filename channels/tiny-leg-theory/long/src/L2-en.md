# L2 Your Phone vs Your Focus

### s06 [buff] Why You Check Your Phone Mid-Task — cue-triggered checking
- Your hand grabbed the phone. Your brain wasn't even asked.
- Phone on the desk? That's bait.
- See it, grab it, tiny reward. Repeat. Habit.
- Move the bait. Other room. Face down.
- Check it on breaks, like rest between sets.
- Flip your phone face down. Right now.

### s07 [pen] The One-Minute Scroll — variable reward
- Just one more video. Sound familiar?
- Forty minutes later...
- Every swipe might be boring, or might be amazing.
- Not knowing what's next keeps you checking.
- Add a stopping cue. A timer, or log out after.
- What's your stopping cue?

### s08 [buff] Why Notifications Break Your Focus — task switching cost
- That buzz cost you way more than a second.
- You read it. You think about it...
- Then, wait. Where was I?
- Every switch back burns a little focus.
- Focus block? Mute what isn't urgent.
- Mute one group chat. Now. I'll wait.

### s09 [pen] Boredom Is Not an Emergency — novelty seeking
- The moment work gets boring...
- your thumb files an escape request.
- Your brain loves new things, so boring feels urgent.
- It isn't. It's just a wave.
- Let the boring minute pass, then decide.
- That boring minute just passed. You're fine.

### s10 [buff] Make Your Phone Boring Again — stimulus control
- Your home screen is a tiny casino.
- Bright icons. Red dots. Endless feeds.
- Every one of them says: tap me.
- Kick the tempting apps off page one.
- Put your tools there instead.
- Move one app off your home screen. Right now.

### s37 [pen] Why Your Brain Hates a Messy Desk — Visual competition
- You sit down to read, and the receipts start talking.
- Mugs, wrappers, an old to-do list, all in view.
- Everything in sight quietly competes for your attention.
- So focusing takes a little more effort than it should.
- You don't need a clean room. Just a clear patch.
- What if the only thing in front of you was the page?

### s38 [buff] Train Next to Someone Who Trains — Social facilitation (works best for simple, well-practiced tasks)
- Funny how you run faster when someone's on the next treadmill.
- Just having people around tends to rev you up.
- It's called social facilitation.
- Catch: it helps easy, practiced stuff. Reps you know.
- New, hard skill? An audience can make you sloppier.
- So bring a buddy for the boring, simple work.
- Inbox, flashcards, laundry. Side by side, no chatting.
- Hard new stuff? Solo set first. Then bring the crowd.

### s39 [pen] Why a Head Start Keeps You Going — Endowed progress effect
- A coffee card that comes with two free stamps already on it.
- Oddly, you're more likely to fill that one.
- Feeling already started tends to pull you forward.
- A blank checklist, though, feels like standing at zero.
- So count what you've already done toward the goal.
- The notes you took, the articles you read. Tick them.
- How far along are you already, without having noticed?

### s40 [buff] Don't Let Email Pick Your Day — Attention capture
- Inbox first thing? Congrats, everyone else just wrote your to-do list.
- New messages grab attention whether they matter or not.
- An hour later, ten people answered. Your thing? Untouched.
- Warm-up rule: pick your top task before opening anything.
- One rep on it. Then the inbox can have you.
- Your list or theirs. Pick before you tap.

### s41 [pen] Why You Lose Your Place After a Ping — Interruption recovery (resumption cue)
- One quick reply, and your half-written paragraph goes blank.
- The message ends. The thought you were holding often doesn't.
- Your mind has to rebuild where you were, and that takes effort.
- So before you reply, leave yourself a marker.
- Half a sentence is enough. Next: add the second example.
- Coming back is easier when you leave the door open.

### s42 [buff] Put a Wall Around Deep Work — Boundary management
- You open the textbook. Someone yells your name. Again.
- Your focus needs a fence. Right now it's an open field.
- Tell people your block. Busy till four, all yours after.
- Then guard it. Phone face down. Door closed.
- No fence, no gains. Announce your block now.

### s43 [pen] Why Endless Feeds Have No Last Page — Missing stopping cues (no natural end point)
- You meant to watch one clip. The feed never said the end.
- A book ends a chapter. A show ends an episode.
- Those small endings are where we usually pause and decide.
- An endless feed removes them. There's no natural place to stop.
- So you keep going, not because you chose to, but because nothing asked.
- You can build the chapter breaks back in.
- Every few clips, lock the screen. Then choose: stop or continue.
- A pause is just a page break you made yourself.

### s44 [buff] Turn Off the Red Dot — Salience bias
- Seventeen red dots. Zero of them are your problem.
- That red dot is a tiny alarm. Your eyes go straight to it.
- Bright, red, in the corner. Your brain tags it as important.
- Open it. A sale. Or a like. Sure, super urgent.
- Settings. Notifications. Badges off for everything that isn't people.
- Keep dots only for messages that actually matter.
- One set, two minutes. Kill the dots now.

### s45 [pen] Why You Keep the App on Page One — Mere exposure
- Your thumb finds the video app before your eyes do.
- It's been on page one for years. You see it every unlock.
- Things we see often tend to feel friendly and easy to pick.
- So your first screen quietly leans toward your oldest habits.
- Move it into a folder on the last page. Add a little effort.
- Put something you'd rather open in its old spot.
- What is your home screen inviting you to do?

### s46 [buff] Give Your Phone a Parking Spot — Object location cue
- Dinner. Phone next to the plate. A check between every bite.
- Within reach means within habit. Your hand doesn't ask permission.
- Give it a parking spot. One place. Same place, every time.
- Shelf by the door. Charger in the hallway. Not your pocket.
- Working or eating, the phone stays parked.
- Pick the spot today. Park it before the next meal. Rep one.

### s47 [pen] Why You Check "Just in Case" — Fear of missing out
- You check again. Maybe something new. Maybe not.
- Not knowing can feel like you're missing out on something.
- Each check soothes it for a moment, then the itch comes back.
- Try fixed check-in times instead. Noon, five, after dinner.
- Most things that matter will still be there at noon.

### s48 [buff] Don't Let Autoplay Coach You — Default effect
- You opened one lecture. Now you're watching a raccoon steal cereal.
- The next video started because you didn't stop it.
- Autoplay is a default. Defaults tend to win when we do nothing.
- Doing nothing isn't neutral. Someone else picked that setting.
- Fix the setting before the session, not during.
- Autoplay off. One flick. Warm-up done.
- Now the video ends, and you decide what's next.
- You're the coach now. Flip the switch before you press play.

### s49 [pen] Why the Group Chat Feels Urgent — Social notification pressure
- The group chat buzzes eleven times while you're on shift.
- It can feel like a summons, like everyone is waiting on you.
- Most of it is memes and plans for next week.
- Still, a buzz from friends tends to pull harder than one from an app.
- Mute the chats that aren't urgent.
- Tell the group: I read the chat at lunch and after six.
- Would they really mind if you answered a little later?

### s50 [buff] Set a Scroll Exit Before You Start — Precommitment
- Waiting for the bus. Just a few clips. Sure, buddy.
- Once the feed grabs you, stopping is the heaviest rep.
- Decide the exit while you're still fresh. Before the first swipe.
- Bus arrives, phone goes away. That's the deal.
- Make it hard to break. Phone in the bag, zipped.
- Set your exit before you start. Or the feed sets it for you.

### s51 [pen] The Phone Check After a Good Moment — Attentional residue
- Perfect sunset. And you're checking who viewed your story.
- Part of your mind stays on the phone after you put it down.
- That leftover pull is called attentional residue.
- So you're half at the sunset, half in the comments.
- Take one photo, then put the phone away until it ends.
- Post it later. Which moment are you actually here for?

### s52 [buff] Open the Scary Bill — Ostrich effect (avoiding unwelcome information)
- Bank app notification. You swipe it away. Classic.
- Not looking feels safer. That's the ostrich effect.
- The number doesn't shrink while you hide from it.
- One rep: open it, read the total, close it.
- Scary for ten seconds, or scary all month. Pick one.

### s53 [pen] Why Everyone's Highlight Reel Stings — Upward social comparison
- Sunday night. Someone your age just bought a house.
- Suddenly your whole week feels small.
- We tend to measure ourselves against people who seem ahead.
- That's upward comparison. And feeds mostly show the best cuts.
- You're comparing your whole day to their best ten seconds.
- Try comparing yourself to you, three months ago.
- Their reel is edited. Your progress just isn't posted.

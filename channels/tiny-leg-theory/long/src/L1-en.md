# L1 Why You Procrastinate (and How to Actually Start)

### s01 [pen] Why You Keep Putting It Off — procrastination as short-term mood repair
- You're not avoiding the task.
- You're avoiding how it feels.
- Boring, scary, or just too big.
- So your brain grabs something that feels better right now.
- Shrink the first step until it's too small to fear.
- What's the tiniest version of your task?

### s02 [buff] The Two-Minute Start Trick — lowering perceived effort to start
- An hour of work? That's a heavy lift, buddy.
- Do a two-minute warm-up instead.
- Too small for your brain to argue with.
- Open the file. One line. One rep.
- The first rep is usually the heaviest.
- Stop at two minutes? Fine. You still showed up.

### s03 [pen] Why Easy Tasks Feel Hard to Start — task aversiveness
- This email takes five minutes.
- So why does it feel enormous?
- Because one part of it is unpleasant.
- Maybe it's an awkward reply, or a tiny decision.
- Name that part, and do just that part first.
- What's the ugly part of your task?

### s04 [buff] Your Deadline Is Doing the Motivating — temporal discounting
- Pressure didn't make you sharper. It just made you move.
- The deadline finally got close. That's all.
- Far rewards whisper. Near ones yell.
- So drag the deadline closer.
- Checkpoint tomorrow. Not next month.
- Put tomorrow's checkpoint in your calendar. Go.

### s05 [pen] Perfectionism Is a Delay Machine — fear of failure, avoidance
- If it can't be perfect...
- your brain votes for not yet.
- Not yet feels safe. Nobody can judge a blank page.
- So make a rough version on purpose.
- Ugly drafts can be fixed. Empty pages can't.
- What will you draft badly today?

### s13 [pen] Motivation Doesn't Come First — action before motivation
- I'll start when I feel motivated.
- Waiting to feel ready is very comfortable.
- But often the feeling shows up after you start.
- Start small, and let your mood catch up.
- And if it doesn't? You still moved.
- What will you start before you feel ready?

### s14 [buff] The Discipline Myth — situation beats willpower
- You don't need more willpower.
- You need the snacks off your desk.
- Easy and visible usually wins.
- Snacks out. Water bottle in.
- Fix the room, not your mood.
- Keep the snacks on the desk if you want. Just don't blame your willpower.

### s31 [pen] The Task You Keep Dodging — Attentional control
- Your eyes keep sliding off the hard part of the page.
- Inbox. Calendar. Snack drawer. Anywhere but here.
- Holding attention on something unclear takes real effort.
- So the mind drifts toward whatever is easier to look at.
- Write the next tiny action on paper, in plain words.
- When you drift, just return to that one line. Nothing more.

### s32 [buff] Say Your Goal to One Person — Social accountability (public commitment to a specific person)
- Starting Monday, you said. Sure, buddy.
- A promise only you know about is easy to skip.
- Tell one person. Not the whole internet. One.
- Be specific. What, and by when.
- Ask them to check in on that exact day.
- Knowing someone will ask tends to keep you moving.
- Text your spotter now. One sentence. Go.

### s33 [pen] Why You Keep Rechecking the Same Thing — Uncertainty reduction
- You sent the email. Then opened Sent to check. Twice.
- Each check brings a small, warm wave of relief.
- But the relief fades quickly, and the doubt asks again.
- Before you check, decide: one careful look, then done.
- The fifth look rarely finds what the first one missed.

### s34 [buff] Pick a Finish Line, Not a Vibe — Goal-setting theory
- Today's plan: work on it. Work on what, exactly?
- That's not a goal. That's a vibe.
- No finish line, no way to know the set is done.
- So you quit early, or grind all night.
- Specific goals tend to beat 'just do your best'.
- Name one result you can see when it's done.
- Not 'study'. Ten flashcards. Not 'write'. The intro.
- Write the finish line down. Then lift.

### s35 [pen] The Open Tab in Your Head — Zeigarnik effect
- You're on the sofa with a movie, but part of you is still at work.
- That unanswered email keeps tapping you on the shoulder.
- Unfinished tasks tend to stay half-open in the mind.
- Like a browser tab you can't quite close.
- You don't have to finish it to quiet it.
- Write down the next step, and when you'll do it.
- With a plan on paper, the mind often lets the tab rest.

### s36 [buff] Make the First Move Obvious — Choice architecture
- Laptop open. Ten minutes later, still hunting for the file.
- Hidden first step? Easy to stall.
- Lifters load the bar before the set. Do the same.
- Tonight, open the exact file, page, and tool. Leave them up.
- Tomorrow you sit down, and the first rep is waiting. Go.

### s62 [buff] Build a Habit That Survives Busy Days — Minimum viable action (a floor version for hectic days)
- Busy day, so the habit takes the day off. Sure, buddy.
- Skip one day and the next skip gets easier.
- Set a floor. The smallest rep you'd still do on your worst day.
- Ten push-ups is the plan. One push-up is the floor.
- Hit the floor today. The full set can wait for a quiet day.

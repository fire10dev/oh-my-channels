# L7 Stress, Worry and Overthinking

### s11 [pen] Why You Replay Awkward Moments at Night — rumination
- Lights off. Eyes closed.
- And your brain plays an awkward moment from years ago.
- Quiet moments give old worries room to loop.
- Name it: that's just a replay.
- Then give your brain a small, boring job, like counting back from one hundred.
- What's your brain's favourite rerun?

### s12 [buff] The 'One More' Bedtime Loop — present bias, bedtime procrastination
- One more video, then sleep. Sure, buddy.
- You're spending tomorrow's energy tonight.
- Fun is right here. Tired is far away. Until morning.
- Pick a shutdown time.
- Phone sleeps outside the bedroom. Done.
- Tomorrow you will say thanks. Probably.

### s25 [pen] Why Future You Feels Like a Stranger — future-self continuity
- Tomorrow you will totally do it.
- Your brain thinks about future you almost like another person.
- So you hand them all the hard stuff.
- Make it easy for them. Set things up tonight.
- Clothes out. Bag packed. First step written.
- What can you do tonight for tomorrow you?

### s121 [pen] Why Naming a Feeling Can Help — Affect labeling
- You say you feel bad. But bad is a big, blurry word.
- Blurry feelings tend to feel bigger, and harder to hold.
- Putting a feeling into words can turn its volume down a little.
- Try one precise word. Nervous. Disappointed. Left out.
- A named feeling is easier to pick a next step for.
- Not bad. Just nervous. That is something you can work with.

### s122 [buff] Don't Reply at Peak Heat — Emotion regulation: response modulation
- Angry text, thumbs already flying. Hold that send button.
- At peak heat, your words land heavier than you mean.
- Draft it. Don't send it. Park it for a minute.
- Cool down. Reread. Cut every line you'd regret.
- You rest between sets. Same rule before you hit send.

### s123 [pen] Why a Small Pause Changes the Moment — Cognitive reappraisal
- Stuck in a slow line, and suddenly the whole evening feels ruined.
- The line didn't ruin it. The story 'this is wasted time' did.
- Feelings tend to follow how you read a moment, not just the moment.
- That's reappraisal: same event, a different reading that's still honest.
- So pause and ask: what else could this moment be?
- Five quiet minutes. Time for the call you keep putting off.
- The line is just as slow. It just weighs a little less.

### s124 [buff] Turn a Vague Worry Into a Next Step — Problem-focused coping
- Worried about the presentation. Again. Fifth hour running.
- Worry feels like work. It's not a rep.
- If you can act on it, name the action.
- Not 'fix the presentation.' Too heavy for one set.
- Smaller. Find two sources. Tonight, after dinner.
- Write the step and the time. Tick it when done.
- Can't act on it yet? Park it on tomorrow's list.
- Worry, or one rep. Pick the rep.

### s125 [pen] Why You Feel Worse After a Long Day — Mood-congruent memory
- After a long shift, you decide you got nothing done all week.
- Tired moods tend to pull up tired memories first.
- So the week gets judged by its worst bits, at your lowest point.
- Save the big verdict on your week for after you've rested.
- Tonight you're tired. That's the only conclusion tonight gets to make.

### s126 [buff] Stop Treating Every Mistake Like a Crisis — Catastrophizing
- Sent the wrong file to the team. Career over, right?
- Your brain jumps from one mistake straight to the worst ending.
- Say the real consequence. Plainly. Out loud.
- They saw an old draft for ten minutes. That's it.
- Then find the repair. Send the right one, plus one line of sorry.
- A missed rep isn't a ruined program. Next rep.

### s127 [pen] The Good Moment You Almost Missed — Attentional bias
- You walked home tonight. Can you name one thing you saw?
- Most of the walk went to replaying one awkward email.
- Attention picks which details get noticed, and it leans toward problems.
- The good bits still happened. They just never got recorded.
- So on the way home, pause once.
- Name one concrete thing going well. The rain stopped. Warm bread smell.
- Small enough to miss. Real enough to count.

### s128 [buff] Use a Wind-Down Cue, Not Another Scroll — Conditioned cue
- Your day ends the same way every night. Lights off, scroll on.
- So your brain never gets a clear 'day is done' signal.
- Cues work when they repeat. Same steps, same order, every night.
- Build a short offline routine. A cool-down set for your day.
- Shower. Phone on the charger, out of reach. Three pages of a book.
- Repeat it enough, and the routine itself starts saying 'day's over.'
- Every workout gets a cool-down. Your day gets one too. Starting tonight.

### s129 [pen] Why Your Explanation Only Made Sense to You — Curse of knowledge
- You explained it twice. They nodded. Nobody understood.
- Once you know something, it's hard to remember not knowing it.
- That's the curse of knowledge. You skip steps you no longer see.
- Your shorthand sounds obvious to you, and like code to them.
- Explain it to a friend outside your field. Watch where they frown.
- Which step did you skip because it felt too obvious to say?

### s130 [buff] Don't Negotiate With the Snooze Button — Immediate reward bias
- Alarm rings. Five more minutes. Sure, buddy.
- Snooze pays you now. Your morning pays the bill.
- Half-asleep you wins every negotiation. So don't negotiate.
- Put the alarm across the room. To stop it, you stand up.
- Pick your first move the night before. Feet down, lights on.
- First rep of the day: get up. Tomorrow.

# L3 Habits That Actually Stick

### s15 [pen] Why Your New Habit Keeps Disappearing — cue-dependent habits
- You made a goal: read more.
- Two weeks later, it's gone.
- Habits need a cue, a place to live.
- Tie it to something you already do.
- After my morning coffee, I read one page.
- What will your habit be attached to?

### s16 [buff] Build a Habit With an If-Then Plan — implementation intentions
- 'I'll exercise more' is a wish, not a plan.
- Try if-then.
- If I close my laptop, then shoes on.
- The cue does the deciding for you.
- Specific beats hyped.
- Drop your if-then in the comments. I'm checking.

### s17 [pen] Missing One Day Isn't a Reset — habits build with repetition
- Missed one day? The streak is broken.
- So you quit the whole thing.
- Habits build by repeating in the same setting, not by perfect streaks.
- One missed day doesn't erase the others.
- Just come back at the next cue.
- Which habit will you restart today?

### s18 [buff] Make the Good Habit Easier to See — salience and environmental cues
- Your dumbbells live under the bed. Bold strategy.
- Now your brain has to remember them twice.
- Out of sight, out of mind. Very real.
- Put the good stuff where you'll trip over it.
- Book on the pillow. Shoes by the door.
- Go put one thing in plain sight. Now.

### s30 [buff] Why Tiny Wins Feel So Good — progress monitoring, goal gradient
- Ticking one box? Feels great.
- Your brain loves proof you're moving.
- Big goals are too far away to feel.
- So track the small reps you control.
- Pages read. Walks done. Not just the finish line.
- Pick one tiny thing. Track it this week.

### s54 [buff] Make the Phone a Tool Again — Goal-directed attention
- Unlocked to check the weather. Forty minutes later: cat videos.
- No job named, so the feed picks one for you.
- Attention follows a goal. No goal? Loudest thing wins.
- Warm-up rule: say the job before you unlock.
- 'Reply to Mom.' Then do exactly that.
- Job done? Lock it. Set complete.
- Feels dumb out loud. Works anyway.
- Next unlock, name the job. One rep.

### s55 [pen] The Habit That Needs a Better Cue — Context-dependent memory
- Ten days of stretching at home. One trip, and it's gone.
- The habit wasn't only yours. Part of it lived in that room.
- Same mat, same lamp, same hour. The place was doing the reminding.
- So pack a cue that travels: right after you brush your teeth.
- What part of your day comes with you everywhere?

### s56 [buff] Don't Plan Tomorrow While You're Pumped — Hot-cold empathy gap
- 11 PM, fired up. Tomorrow: 5 AM run, read, cold shower.
- 5 AM you: hits snooze. Hard.
- Pumped you can't feel how tired morning you will be.
- That's the hot-cold empathy gap. Hype writes, morning pays.
- Plan for the tired version. Cut the list in half.
- Light plan you actually lift beats a heavy one you skip.

### s57 [pen] Why You Do What Your Friends Do — Descriptive social norms
- New roommate studies every night. Weirdly, now you do too.
- Nobody asked you. Nobody judged you.
- We quietly copy what looks normal around us.
- It works the other way too. Everyone scrolls, you scroll.
- Part of your routine is borrowed from the room.
- So borrow on purpose. Sit near people who do the thing.
- A library seat, a running group, a friend who cooks.
- Whose normal are you borrowing this week?

### s58 [buff] Pair the Boring Set With a Treat — Temptation bundling
- You hate the treadmill. You love that podcast. Interesting.
- Lock them together. That podcast only plays on the treadmill.
- That's temptation bundling. The fun drags the boring along.
- Listen anywhere else? Deal's broken. No cheating.
- Pick one boring set. Pair it tonight.

### s59 [pen] The Habit You Only Do When Inspired — Context stability
- 'I'll read at 8 every night.' Then your class times change.
- Some nights 8 is the bus. Some nights it's dinner.
- Habits settle in when the cue shows up the same way each day.
- A clock time isn't stable if your days aren't.
- But some things always happen. You get home. You lie down.
- Anchor to that. After I lie down, two pages.
- The hour keeps changing. Lying down never skips a night.

### s60 [buff] Make the Bad Habit Inconvenient — Response cost
- Bored at midnight. Two taps. Package on the way.
- Zero friction means the impulse does the whole rep alone.
- That's response cost. Add effort, and the urge often quits first.
- Log out. Delete the saved card. Bury the app in a folder.
- Still want it tomorrow? Fine. Buy it then.
- Make it annoying. Annoying beats sorry.

### s61 [pen] Why You Forget the Habit You Chose — Prospective memory (remembering to act later)
- You decided to drink water at lunch. Lunch came and went.
- The plan was real. The moment just didn't remind you.
- Remembering to do something later is its own kind of memory, and it slips easily.
- A busy day pulls your attention everywhere except the plan.
- So put the reminder where the action happens. Bottle right on the desk.
- Not in your head. In your way.

### s62 [buff] Build a Habit That Survives Busy Days — Minimum viable action (a floor version for hectic days)
- Busy day, so the habit takes the day off. Sure, buddy.
- Skip one day and the next skip gets easier.
- Set a floor. The smallest rep you'd still do on your worst day.
- Ten push-ups is the plan. One push-up is the floor.
- Hit the floor today. The full set can wait for a quiet day.

### s63 [pen] Why Rewards Can Change the Fun — Overjustification effect
- You used to draw for hours, just because you liked it.
- Then you started posting for likes. Now it feels like homework.
- When a reward shows up, the reason you're doing it can quietly shift.
- It has a name: the overjustification effect.
- Not everything you do needs a score.
- Keep one thing you do only because you enjoy it. No tracking, no posting.
- What did you love doing before anyone was counting?

### s64 [buff] One Good Workout Isn't a Free Pass — Self-licensing
- Great workout today. So you've earned three days off. Sure, buddy.
- One good move can start to feel like permission for a bad one.
- It's called self-licensing. Progress turns into credit you get to spend.
- Wrong. The workout wasn't a deposit. It was a rep.
- Say it like identity: I'm someone who trains.
- Book the next session before you leave the gym.
- Wins are fuel for the next rep, not a pass to skip it.
- Put tomorrow on the calendar now. Then go celebrate.

### s65 [pen] Why Your Routine Breaks on Weekends — Context disruption (weekday cues disappear)
- Weekdays, you read right after your alarm. Saturday, no alarm, no reading.
- The habit didn't vanish. Its cue did.
- Weekday habits often lean on weekday cues: the alarm, the commute, the desk.
- So give the weekend its own cue, like the first coffee, whenever it happens.
- Maybe the habit was never lazy. It was waiting for an alarm that never rang.

### s66 [buff] Put a Pause Between Urge and Click — Response inhibition
- Flash sale pops up. Your thumb is already on Buy.
- Urge shows up. You don't owe it a rep.
- Urges run on autopilot. A short pause gives the brakes time to kick in.
- Phone face down. Count to ten, slowly.
- Still want it after ten? Fine. Now you're choosing, not twitching.
- Next urge, you make the first move. Ten count. Go.

### s67 [pen] Good Decision, Bad Luck — Outcome bias
- You packed an umbrella. It didn't rain. Now it feels like a dumb call.
- We tend to grade a decision by how it turned out.
- That's outcome bias. Luck sneaks into the score.
- A good choice can still meet a bad day. A sloppy one can get lucky.
- So judge the process first: did you use what you knew at the time?
- If yes, keep the plan. Only fix what you could have known.
- The umbrella was still right. The sky just didn't play along.

### s68 [buff] Make the Next Rep Easier to Repeat — Self-efficacy (repeatable small wins)
- Day one: a heroic hour-long workout. Days two to ten: couch.
- Hero sessions feel great once. Then they feel impossible to repeat.
- Confidence grows from wins you actually finish. Again and again.
- Start with a load you could lift on a bad day.
- Three easy sessions this week beat one legendary one.
- Finish easy today. Add a little next week. That's the program.

### s69 [pen] Why You Need a Fresh Start Monday — Fresh-start effect
- Every Sunday night you say it: Monday, I start for real.
- A new week, a new month, a birthday. They feel like a clean page.
- That feeling can help. It puts a little distance between you and old slips.
- The trap is the wait. Thursday turns into a waiting room for Monday.
- And by Monday, the energy has often gone into planning.
- Use the reset feeling, but take the first step today.
- Not the big launch. One small, quiet move.
- What could you start before the calendar gives you permission?

### s70 [buff] Stop Waiting to Feel Confident — Self-efficacy through mastery experiences
- I'll present when I feel confident. Sure, buddy. That day isn't coming.
- Confidence doesn't show up first. It shows up after reps.
- Your brain trusts evidence: things you've actually done.
- Pick a challenge that's slightly scary. One step past easy.
- Do it. Then write down that you did it.
- Stack enough proof and the nerves usually get quieter.
- Today: one small scary rep. Then log it.

### s71 [pen] Why the First Draft Feels So Personal — IKEA effect (valuing what you made)
- You spent three nights on this draft. Now cut a paragraph.
- Suddenly it feels like cutting a piece of you.
- We tend to value things more simply because we built them.
- The effort quietly gets mixed up with the quality.
- So a small note can sound like a verdict on you.
- Try calling it version one. A working object, not a portrait.
- The nights still count. They just don't have to stay in the draft.

### s72 [buff] Aim for Practice, Not a Perfect Score — Mastery goal orientation
- Full mock exam. Again. Same score. Again.
- You're testing every day. You're not training.
- A score goal only asks one thing: am I good yet?
- A practice goal asks: which move gets better today?
- Pick one weak spot. Ten clean reps of it.
- Reps show up tonight. The score shows up later.
